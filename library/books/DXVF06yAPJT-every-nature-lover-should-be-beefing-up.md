Every nature lover should be beefing up their knowledge! Here are some recommendations. #trees #desert #environmentalism #publicland #activism

Top five books that you should read about public lands issues. A lot of people really care about this stuff, but they're kind of coaxed into thinking that maybe they're a little naïve because they don't know the science or the policy behind the arguments. These books will make you an effective debater. Coming in at number five, Northwest Trees. A book about all of the trees of the Pacific Northwest and their ecosystems and how these trees operate, how they grow, where they grow, what they contribute to the ecosystem. Number four, the legacy of Luna. This is about Julia Butterfly Hill. She was the badass woman who was like 19 years old who lived in a tree for two years during the earth first movement in the late 90s. She put her life on the line to live in this old growth tree that was going to be cut down. And she lived in the tree during the year of El Nino when there were these crazy storms ripping through the area with no nails in the plank that she was living on. And the plank was 180 feet into this tree. This book really just kind of expresses what it's like to be a land protector and it kind of dangers that you're going to go through. If you want to go actually put your body and your freedom on the line to fight for it. Desert Solitaire. Desert Solitaire is about the author Edward Abbey and how he spent a couple of years as a park ranger at Arches National Park in Moab. The thing with Edward Abbey is that kind of a problematic figure, great writer. He makes a lot of great points as far as environmentalism and our public lands goes. He also says some pretty racist shit in this book about indigenous people. And so it's kind of about his life while he's out there and how the national parks themselves are building tons of roads through the national parks and destroying ecosystems. This land, this land is by Christopher Ketchum. I don't know if anybody else had written something so all-encompassing about our public lands before this. For me it's kind of the modern day desert solitaire. He talks about all of the mismanagement and the concessions that all of our public lands management agencies have made to lead us to where we're at now. So this was a foundation that was laid from administration to administration, including the democratic administrations. This book really shows you these are deep rooted issues that have been there since the birth of modern conservation with Gifford Pinchot, the founder of the Forest Service. Number one, Cadillac Desert by Mark Reisner. So this book is about water use policies all over the country. It starts in Los Angeles in the early 1900s. When you read this book you realize that everything goes back to the watershed, that we are all part of the same watershed. What this book does is it really shows you the importance of our water use policies, where they came from and how most of our rivers wound up behind dams. And how that then flows into the energy companies, which flows into the development. Even if you read the first 250 pages, you're going to get a lot of information out of it. Take notes.
