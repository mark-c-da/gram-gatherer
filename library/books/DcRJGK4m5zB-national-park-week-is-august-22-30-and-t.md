## Raw

National Park Week is August 22–30, and this year’s theme is “Celebrate America’s Story,” honoring the country’s 250th anniversary and the National Park Service’s 110th birthday. But as we celebrate these public lands, their future remains uncertain. 

In late July, President Donald Trump signed proclamations reducing Utah’s Bears Ears and Grand Staircase–Escalante National Monuments by 90 percent to make way for oil drilling and uranium mining. Developments like this are among the most critical threats facing public lands, putting future generations’ access to the outdoors at risk.

We invited @dillon.osleger, scientist, environmental advocate, and author of Trail Work: Restoring the Paths and Stories of America’s Public Lands, to share five books that contextualize how America laid claim to these natural areas, who owned them first, the threats facing public lands today, and how to get involved in environmental advocacy.

Here are five books to start with:

📖 Grinnell: America’s Environmental Pioneer and His Restless Drive to Save the West, by John Taliaferro
📖 This Contested Land: The Storied Past and Uncertain Future of America’s National Monuments, by McKenzie Long
📖 Dispossessing the Wilderness: Indian Removal and the Making of the National Parks, by Mark David Spence
📖 Our Common Ground: A History of America’s Public Lands, by John D. Leshy
📖 The High Sierra of California, by Gary Snyder and illustrated by @tomkillion
